### Executive Summary & Project Goal

Fiction Hall (deployed internally as `nocative`) is a reader/creator platform for writing, publishing, discovering, reading, and selling collections of chapters. Text-only prose, illustrated prose, and image-led comics should remain viable first-class formats; Markdown with externally linked media is the present authoring model. Creators set collection prices; buyers purchase either one-year or permanent access with Stripe card checkout or selected Arbitrum stablecoins. Reader-return features (`/fav`, `/bookmark`) aim to make stories emotionally worth revisiting, not merely add dashboard activity. No working subscription is part of the intended current business model, despite legacy subscription/plan tables and endpoints in the code.

### Core Feature Set

- Creators register, edit profile (display name, introduction, optional contact, X/Reddit/Substack handles), connect/disable/re-enable/disconnect Stripe, and add/remove an Arbitrum EVM wallet at signup or later. Public author pages show created collections and up to 50 browsed notes; consider the privacy implications of that reading trace.
- A collection can be published for reading without a payout rail. Sale requires at least one eligible rail: fully usable Stripe Connect **or** an enabled, valid writer Arbitrum wallet with crypto configured. No rail means no sale or fallback background checkout. Stripe and crypto availability are independent; gifts are **Stripe-only** and require usable creator Stripe status for creator-directed gifts.
- Chapters can be free or paid, drafts or live. Accessible reads increment `reader_chapter_read_count`; locked previews do not count. `/fav` is a private, frequency-ranked bookshelf, not a public leaderboard. `/bookmark` publishes selected passage plus required reflection and links back to chapter/collection/author; viewers cannot identify the bookmarking reader, though D1 retains ownership for “mine.”
- Collection rental means one-year access; permanent unlock persists. `story_unlock` upsert protects permanent access from later rental overwrites. Live streaming/chat and Stripe gifts exist; they are separate from collection unlock economics.

### Payment Infrastructure

- **Stripe unlock:** Creator sets fiat prices, subject to $14 rental/$21 permanent floor in intended checkout behavior (inspect each route for consistent enforcement). Buyer pays listed fiat price. Destination-charge Checkout directs proceeds to a connected creator and takes a 5% platform application fee on rentals or 10% on permanent sales (creator 95%/90%), before Stripe fees. `/api/stripe/unlock-webhook` creates `story_unlock` and `purchase` after `checkout.session.completed`; success-URL query text alone does not grant access. Stripe gifts use separate checkout/webhook routes; creator gift and optional platform gift amounts are explicit, not an automatic percentage.
- **Crypto quote:** Worker requires an authenticated buyer, sellable collection, enabled writer wallet, configured contract/signer/token addresses and RPC. Supported token identities are native Arbitrum USDC, USDT0 (stored internally as `USDT` in quotes), and DAI—not USDC.e. Rental buyer price is 0.7× fiat; permanent buyer price 0.5× fiat. `splitA` pays writer 85%/treasury 15%; `splitB` writer 80%/treasury 20%. The contract also exposes fixed `splitC`–`splitG` (22%, 23%, 11%, 8%, 3.5% platform) and signed-basis-point `splitCustom`, but the checkout Worker currently calls only A/B. These fixed platform percentages are not mutable post-deployment; a signed custom call is a distinct path, not a silent change to A/B.
- **QR/settlement:** Quote lasts 15 minutes. Two `ethereum:` contract-call URIs/QRs represent ERC-20 approval and `splitA`/`splitB` payment; no app wallet connection. A wallet must support these URIs; MetaMask desktop extension does not register the scheme, and QR generation by itself proves no wallet compatibility. Contract verifies EIP-712 quote, token whitelist, deadline and unused order, then `safeTransferFrom`s buyer directly to writer and treasury and emits `CryptoCustomPurchase`; it does not hold the writer share. The open checkout page polls `/api/crypto/quotes/:id/status` every 3 seconds. Worker scans `eth_getLogs` in inclusive windows of at most 10 blocks (Alchemy Free restriction), verifies matching event **and successful receipt**, then atomically writes unlock, purchase, `crypto_payment_receipt`, and confirmed quote status. This is Arbitrum receipt inclusion, **not** later Ethereum L1 finality or a mempool-signature acknowledgement. There is a validated manual hash-confirm endpoint, but UI no longer requires buyers to paste a hash. No independent background reconciliation is established if the buyer closes the page.
- **D1 crypto evidence:** `crypto_payment_receipt` snapshots quote ID, transaction hash/block UTC timestamp, buyer/seller user IDs and usernames, payer/writer addresses, token symbol/address/decimals, exact total/writer/platform token amounts as base-unit strings, and quoted USD micro-units. The buyer username belongs to the account that created the quote; the payer wallet can belong to someone else. `quoted_usd_amount_e6` and `purchase.fmv` currently reflect the quote/assumed stablecoin value, **not** independently captured market FMV. For tax books, retain contemporaneous token-specific USD FMV, source, and receipt time separately; do not hard-code $1/token or substitute later Ethereum finality time. Tax residence/reporting cannot be inferred solely from citizenship or a 180-day rule; obtain jurisdiction-specific professional advice.

### Key Decisions Made & Architectural Constraints

- Keep direct creator-defined collection purchases, not recurring subscriptions as the operative product model. Do not silently revive legacy `plan`/`subscription` endpoints as the roadmap.
- No wallet-connect second login for crypto checkout. Preserve QR/deep-link concept, but test actual wallet compatibility and show safe fallback instructions. Never unlock on QR generation, wallet signature, submitted hash, or Stripe redirect alone.
- Stripe Connect and writer Arbitrum wallet are independently optional; one valid rail permits sales, neither permits publication only. Gifts stay Stripe-only. Stripe statuses are `null`, `disabled`, `fully connected`; disabling D1 status differs from deleting the Stripe Connect account.
- Crypto is Arbitrum One; token allowlist is native USDC, USDT0, DAI. Writer share transfers directly from payer via contract, with the platform treasury as separate recipient. Do not substitute custodial escrow or a different chain without explicit approval. Contract is non-upgradeable; current A/B economics are fixed.
- Preserve exact price/split distinctions: **fiat $14/$21 floors, 95%/90% Stripe creator share; crypto reader multipliers 0.7/0.5 and writer shares 85%/80%**. Reader discount and creator split are different parameters.
- Treat this as an emotional media product: improve reading, revisiting, and creator-reader connection without forcing gimmicks or excluding comics. `/fav` and public anonymous bookmarks exist; proposed songs for weekly writers, a 3D spine, built-in comic storage, and name changes are not committed features.

### What Has Failed or Been Explicitly Ruled Out

- Earlier crypto status polling returned HTTP 503 because Alchemy Free rejects `eth_getLogs` ranges wider than 10 blocks on Arbitrum. Current scan chunks to 10; upgrading to paid Alchemy was **not required** for that fix. Recheck quota/throughput before scale claims.
- Generic `ethereum:` links failed to launch in desktop browsers without a registered handler; do not claim a QR works end-to-end until a real mobile-wallet test. No real-money E2E checkout has been validated yet.
- Requiring buyers to manually paste a transaction hash was rejected; receipt/event polling is the implemented unlock path. Do not mistake “automatic while page polls” for a background listener.
- Prior Save moment selection instability included capturing an overlong range from chapter start; the latest fix defers capture until selection ends, but still needs a human/browser regression test on the reported passage.
- `contracts/README.md` is stale; it mentions three price feeds, configurable deployed splits, and an old contract/script. Current Solidity takes treasury/signer/token addresses at deployment, has no oracle feeds, and A/B percentages are fixed. Never reconstruct deployment arguments from that README.
- No RGB-array-on-blockchain image storage, no configured R2 comic uploader, no automatic 3D bookshelf requirement, and no verified claim that Stripe/crypto payment architecture alone removes US LLC tax or reporting duties. Do not expose keys or private wallet material in docs/logs.
